# SFX & audio dictionary

Vocabulary for the `SFX:` part of SHOT lines and for location ambience beds. It's a starting vocabulary, not a closed list; use plain words for anything missing. The script's own sound cues always come first, rewritten as present-tense nouns ("the <source> <verb>s" → "<source> <verb>").

## Layering order
Each shot's SFX lists only sounds that happen in that shot, most important first:
1. **Hard FX**: the key event sound (impact, door, explosion).
2. **Foley**: body and object sounds (footsteps, cloth, handling).
3. **Music / score**: only if the script calls for it.

The **ambience bed** (room tone, crowd, weather) is part of the location lock and is stated once in ENVIRONMENT; mention it in SFX only when it changes.

## Audio techniques
Write these as plain words inside SFX (`sudden silence`, `footsteps moving left to right`); the slash prefix is for camera tokens only. The edit-only terms (j-cut, l-cut, sound bridge) go in edit notes.

- silence: Total absence of sound.
- hard audio cut: Sound drops instantly to silence (or silence to full sound).
- room tone: Near-silent natural background of a space.
- muffled: Sound dulled as if underwater, behind a wall, or dazed.
- echo / reverb: Sound reflecting in a large or hard space.
- distorted: Sound warped, crushed or glitching.
- riser: Sound rising in pitch and volume toward a hit.
- downer: Sound falling in pitch after a hit.
- sub-bass hit: Deep low-frequency impact felt more than heard.
- reverse swell: Reversed sound building into a moment.
- whoosh: Air movement passing the camera.
- stinger: Short sharp accent on a reveal.
- tinnitus ring: High ringing after a blast or shock.
- heartbeat: Low rhythmic thump for tension.
- breath: Close audible breathing.
- j-cut: Next shot's sound starts before the cut.
- l-cut: Current shot's sound continues after the cut.
- sound bridge: One sound carried across a transition.
- diegetic: Sound that exists in the scene.
- non-diegetic: Sound only the audience hears (score, narration).
- spatial pan left / right: Sound placed or moving across the stereo field.
- distant: Sound far away, softer with more air.
- close-mic: Sound very near and intimate.

## Music cues
- Mood: tense, triumphant, melancholic, mysterious, playful, epic, eerie, romantic, uplifting, ominous.
- Shape: swell, build, drop, sting, fade out, hard stop, silence, loop.
- Texture: strings, choir, piano, synth pad, drums, percussion hits, solo instrument, orchestral, electronic, lo-fi.

## Voice processing
